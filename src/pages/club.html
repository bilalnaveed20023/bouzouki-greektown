IThe Main Stage

The middle of the room.

The main stage is the biggest thing in the building: a long, lit stage with three poles, set out on the floor with seats right up against it. Wherever you sit in the room, you are looking at it.

Sets rotate through the night, so whenever you walk in the room is already going. Take a seat at the stage edge for the closest view in the house.

Tipping the stage

Bring singles for the stage. It is the easiest way to say thank you, and the entertainers notice who does.

IIThe Second Stage

A stage of its own.

The second stage is raised, ringed in light and run under its own rig, with two poles and a screen overhead. It keeps its side of the room going, so there is always something to watch whichever way your seat faces.

Smaller than the main stage and closer to the tables around it, so the seats near it feel like the front row.

IIIOlympus

Olympus. Your party only.

Off the main floor is Olympus, a private room with its own mini stage: a lit glass platform with a single pole, a mirrored wall and leather couches around low tables. It is a separate room you book for your group, so the show is yours and nobody else’s.

VIP is for couch dances; Olympus is the whole room, for your group alone. Ask your server on the night, or call ahead to book it.

VIThe Kitchen

Hot food at one in the morning.

Detroit closes early and eats late, which is an awkward combination everywhere except here. Our kitchen is on its feet until close: Greek plates from the room’s own history, wings, burgers, and platters built for a table of eight.

It is a genuine kitchen, not a fryer in a corner. The Greek side of the menu is the oldest thing in the building — a direct line back to when 432 E Lafayette was a restaurant with bouzouki players in it.

The name

Why “Bouzouki”?

A bouzouki is a long-necked Greek string instrument — the sound of a taverna at midnight. When this address opened as a Greek restaurant and lounge, that is exactly what it was: live strings, plates coming out of the kitchen, belly dancers working between courses, Greektown at full volume.

The neighbourhood turned over. The casino arrived. The stage changed shape and so did the entertainment. But nobody ever saw a reason to change the sign — and if you look at the club’s mark, the fluted column and the two dancers are still standing exactly where they have always stood.

Greektown’s oldest late room, under its original name.
